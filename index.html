What I can help with

Young people (10+)

Teenagers come to me about anxiety, school, friendships, and feelings that have got too big to carry around. Parents are kept in the loop, with the young person's agreement.

Nature-informed therapy

Some people think better while walking. Outdoor sessions can be arranged locally, and when the weather doesn't play along we bring the same grounding work indoors.

Creative & art-based work

When talking is hard, drawing or making something can carry what words can't. You don't need to be any good at it.

Anxiety, stress & life transitions

Adults come to me during a divorce, a bereavement, burnout, or a stretch of low mood that hasn't lifted on its own.

How I work

I trained integratively, which is a long way of saying I don't put everyone through the same model. In practice I lean on two things fairly heavily. Being outdoors, because a difficult conversation is often easier side by side than face to face. And making things, because some feelings arrive as a drawing long before they arrive as a sentence.

You might be fifteen and fed up. You might be a parent who isn't sure whether they're overreacting. Either way, nothing here is rushed, and you won't be judged for what you bring.

A note on safe practice

I work within the BACP's Ethical Framework for the Counselling Professions and do continuing professional development every year. I'm also in regular clinical supervision, which is a requirement of my professional body rather than an optional extra, and it keeps my work accountable to someone other than me.

Questions I get asked a lot

How many sessions will I need?

It varies enormously. Some people find six or eight sessions enough for a specific problem, others stay for a year or more. We'll review it regularly, and you're never tied in.

Do you offer online sessions?

Yes, most sessions happen over secure video, which teenagers in particular often prefer. In-person can be arranged on request.

What if my teenager doesn't want to talk?

Very common, and not a problem. We go at their pace, and there's usually something to do with our hands while we get there.

Is what we discuss confidential?

Yes, with a small number of legal and safety exceptions. I'll go through those properly at the start so nothing comes as a surprise.

What this form collects

When you submit the contact form, I receive: your name; who the enquiry is for (yourself, a teenager, or someone else); your email address and phone number; whatever you choose to write in the message box; and your consent to me holding that information. The form also records the time it was submitted, which is used only to help tell real enquiries apart from automated spam and isn't personal information about you.

Why I collect it, and my lawful basis

I collect this information for one purpose: to read your enquiry, get back to you, and arrange a first conversation if that's what you'd like. I don't use it for marketing, and I don't share it with anyone for their own purposes.

My lawful basis for processing your name, email, phone number and message is your consent — given when you tick the consent box before sending the form (Article 6(1)(a) UK GDPR). You can withdraw that consent at any time; see "Your rights", below.

A note about health information

Therapy enquiries often touch on mental health, which UK data protection law treats as a "special category" of more sensitive personal data — even a short message like "I've been struggling with anxiety" falls into this category. Where your message includes anything of this kind, my lawful basis for holding it is your explicit consent (Article 9(2)(a) UK GDPR), given in the same way, by ticking the consent box. You're welcome to keep your message as brief or as general as you like; you don't need to explain anything in detail before we've spoken.

If you're enquiring on behalf of a young person

If you're a parent or carer enquiring about a young person aged 10–17, I'll treat the information you provide in the same way as any other enquiry. Once we speak, I'll talk you through how confidentiality works for the young person themselves, and what (if anything) is shared with parents or carers going forward — this is covered in more detail on the free introductory call, and there are legal exceptions to confidentiality that I'll always explain clearly, for example where there's a safeguarding concern.

Who sees your information

Your enquiry reaches my inbox through a small chain of services, each of which only processes it in order to deliver it to me:

  • Cloudflare — runs the small piece of code ("Worker") that receives your form submission and passes it on. Cloudflare doesn't store your enquiry in a database; it relays it and, like most infrastructure providers, keeps short-lived technical logs (such as your IP address) for security and reliability purposes.
  • Brevo — the email delivery service that sends your enquiry to me and handles my reply going back to you. Brevo processes the content of the email in order to send it.
  • Google (Gmail) — where I read and reply to your enquiry, in an inbox only I have access to.

I don't sell, rent, or share your information with anyone else, and no one uses it for their own marketing. Some of these providers operate internationally, so your information may be processed on servers outside the UK; where that happens, it's protected by the standard safeguards UK data protection law requires (such as the UK's International Data Transfer Addendum or an equivalent approved mechanism), which each provider sets out in their own privacy and data processing terms.

How I keep it secure

The form is only ever submitted over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection. There's no database sitting behind this website holding your details — your enquiry exists as an email in my inbox, which only I can access, protected by my Google account's own security. The form also has basic, automated protection against spam bots, described below.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law, you have the right to:

  • ask for a copy of the information I hold about you;
  • ask me to correct anything that's inaccurate or incomplete;
  • ask me to delete your information, or restrict how I use it;
  • object to how I'm using it;
  • ask for your information in a portable format, where that applies; and
  • withdraw your consent at any time, which stops any further use of your information for this purpose (it doesn't undo anything already done with your consent up to that point — for example, an email I've already sent you).

To use any of these rights, email jen@jlyctherapy.com. I'll respond within one month.

Automated checks and spam filtering
